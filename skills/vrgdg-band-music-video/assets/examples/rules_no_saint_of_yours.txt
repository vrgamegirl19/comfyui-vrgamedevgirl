# Music Video Project – Creative Direction & Instructions

## Project Goal

Create a highly cinematic, photorealistic music video using all the supplied assets and our previous music video workflows as references.

I'm providing a main project folder containing subfolders with characters, band members, locations, music, lyrics, style prompts, and individual instrument/vocal stems.

Analyze everything before planning the video. Use our previous music video projects and established prompting techniques to guide the overall production.

The goal is to create a professional music video that alternates between **energetic full-band performances and cinematic solo sequences featuring the lead singer.**

## 1. Music Analysis & Editing

- Analyze the full song, lyrics, musical structure, energy changes, and instrument stems.
- Identify intros, verses, choruses, instrumental breaks, solos, and outros.
- Use energetic sections, particularly choruses and heavy instrumental passages, for band performance sequences.
- Use quieter or more emotional sections for the singer's solo journey.
- Follow the music's rhythm with natural cinematic cuts and transitions.
- Use individual stems to identify exactly when each instrument is active, prominent, or performing a solo.
- Give every band member individual moments to shine, including close-ups of guitar riffs, bass performances, and drumming.
- Match each performance to the actual instrument heard in the music.

## 2. Band Performance – Stage Only

**All band members perform exclusively in the provided stage location.**

Establish a consistent stage layout:

- **Lead singer:** Center stage.
- **Lead guitarist:** Stage left.
- **Bass guitarist:** Stage right.
- **Drummer:** Rear center behind the singer.

Position everyone with realistic spacing, similar to a professional live band. Maintain these positions across all performance shots, allowing natural movement and interaction.

The band should feel alive and authentic:

- Guitarists can move toward each other, perform back-to-back, exchange looks, or interact naturally.
- Band members should move with the rhythm and demonstrate realistic playing techniques.
- Use full-band wide shots, medium shots, dramatic close-ups, side angles, and moving cinematic shots.
- Give each musician dedicated performance shots that highlight their instrument and personality.

**Instrument accuracy is critical.**

Only describe a musician actively playing when their instrument is audible in the corresponding stem.

When an instrument is inactive, describe the musician's actual non-playing behavior, such as resting their hands, moving with the rhythm, or interacting with another musician.

Use positive action descriptions rather than negative instructions.

## 3. Lead Singer – Solo Journey

The lead singer is the only band member who appears outside the stage.

Use **every supplied solo location** throughout the music video, distributing them naturally across the song.

These sequences should feel like a cinematic solo journey rather than a literal recreation of the lyrics.

The singer should:

- Sing directly toward the camera with expressive facial movements and natural lip synchronization.
- Walk through environments and interact with surroundings.
- Explore locations through natural body movements, gestures, and changing emotions.
- Occasionally look away, turn, pause, or engage with environmental features.
- Display a range of expressions matching the energy and emotional tone of the song.

Use varied cinematic framing, including extreme close-ups, medium shots, full-body shots, tracking shots, and smooth orbiting movements.

Avoid repetitive standing-and-singing shots. Make the singer physically active and visually engaging.

## 4. Microphone Rules

**On stage:** The singer uses the same **black handheld microphone** in every performance shot. It is a studio-style handheld microphone, consistently held in her hand.

**Solo locations:** The singer performs naturally without microphone equipment. Her hands remain available for gestures and environmental interactions.

Maintain microphone appearance and continuity throughout every stage performance.

## 5. Vocal & Instrument Synchronization

Use the vocal stem to determine exactly when singing occurs.

- During vocal sections, describe realistic singing, mouth movements, and facial expressions synchronized to the lyrics.
- During instrumental or non-vocal sections, describe appropriate closed-mouth expressions, breathing, emotional reactions, or physical movement.
- Avoid assigning vocal performances to sections where the singer has no vocal activity.
- Match instrument performance descriptions to their corresponding audio stems.
- During instrumental solos, prioritize the musician responsible for the prominent instrument.

All character actions should reflect what is actually happening in the music.

## 6. Cinematic Style & Continuity

The video should feel like a professionally directed music video, with intentional shot composition, visual storytelling, and dynamic editing.

Maintain:

- Exact character identities, facial features, hairstyles, outfits, and instruments.
- Consistent stage architecture and musician positioning.
- Realistic instrument handling and performance movements.
- Natural facial expressions and believable body movements.
- Cohesive visual aesthetics across all supplied locations.
- Varied camera angles and shot types.
- Seamless transitions between solo sequences and band performances.

The solo scenes should feel visually connected, creating an emotional journey through the environments without requiring a literal storyline.

## 7. Prompt Generation Rules

Apply our previous music video prompting techniques and established workflows.

- Write detailed, standalone scene prompts.
- Use positive descriptions of visible actions rather than negative wording.
- Describe specific physical movements instead of generic performance instructions.
- Maintain spatial continuity and consistent subject references.
- Explicitly describe the correct musician, instrument, and action.
- Give the singer meaningful movement and interaction in solo scenes.
- Ensure each scene's actions are appropriate for its exact musical timing.
- Keep every shot visually distinct and purposeful.

## 8. Expected Workflow

1. Examine all project folders and identify every available asset.
2. Analyze the song, lyrics, structure, energy, vocals, and instrument stems.
3. Establish the fixed stage arrangement and character continuity rules.
4. Map out the full music video timeline, identifying band performance sections and solo sequences.
5. Distribute every provided solo location throughout the timeline.
6. Plan dynamic cuts, musical highlights, musician close-ups, and character interactions.
7. Develop cinematic, production-ready prompts for every planned shot using our existing workflow.

## Final Creative Objective

Produce a visually impressive music video that feels like a real rock band's professionally directed production.

The band delivers powerful, authentic performances during the song's heavier and more energetic moments, while the singer takes the audience on an expressive solo journey through all the supplied locations.

**Every band member gets meaningful screen time, every location is used, every performance matches the music, and the entire video feels cohesive, energetic, cinematic, and believable.**
